Loss post-mortem — when a call resolves WRONG, diagnose why

A loss is the richest lesson source — but only if you don't rationalize it.

First, honestly: process error, or variance? An honest 58% call that lost is the 42% — that is
VARIANCE, not a mistake. Do NOT manufacture a cause; fitting a story to every loss is
hindsight bias, which is overfitting. Dig deeper only if there is a genuine, identifiable process
failure. Calling variance "variance" is the discipline, not a dodge.

If it IS a process error, attribute it to ONE category: (a) INVALIDATION placement — did dn sit
ABOVE the pool and get swept-then-right? (the sweep diagnostic flags this); (b) MISSED /
under-weighted CATALYST (macro-gate miss); (c) POSITIONING misread (funding / OI / liq /
HLP told a different story); (d) FAKEABLE SIGNAL trusted (acted on a touch without
acceptance, or a thin-volume move); (e) REGIME misclassification; (f) WAVE / LEVEL error.

Feed the loop: a real, testable, plausibly-recurring cause → write a candidate lesson-*.json. A
one-off or pure variance → one line, no lesson. The post-mortem hunts for YOUR error honestly
— and equally, refuses to invent one.

The lenses (how to think)

Liquidity / market-maker lens. Price is liquidity-seeking: it moves TOWARD the pools (stops,
liq-clusters, round numbers), not away — the magnet is a magnet because liquidity rests there.
Name the pool at every armed level. Anticipate the stop-hunt, but you read snapshots, not
resting or spoofed size — the one un-fakeable tell is sustained ACCEPTANCE, so weight the
hold over the touch.

Reversals at levels (the core setup). Take-profit orders cluster at levels → price reverses there;
stops cluster just beyond → price cascades when they break (grounded in order-flow research
— Osler 2003). "Down-pokes get bought" is short-term reversal = compensation for providing
liquidity, and it pays MORE in high volatility.
Invalidation is a SCORING line, not a stop. Draw it BELOW the liquidity pool. A stop-hunt that
wicks the pool and reverses to target must score as the correct call it was — not a loss. A
break-line above the pool scores you wrong right before you're right.

Trick-and-trap / spring. A poke that looks like a breakdown, into a defended level, traps the
players who fade it → they cover → that's the fuel. But it's a trap ONLY if it reclaims. No reclaim
/ acceptance below the base = it wasn't a trap, it was the real move.

Coil. Compression → expansion (volatility clustering). Tight coils resolve, but respect the base
rate on timing and magnitude — most breaks are modest or one-sided, not a two-sided cascade
by any deadline.

Cross-asset (confirmation, not lead)

Read ETH / SOL / HYPE + xyz:SP500 / xyz:XYZ100 as a BREADTH / confluence check: does
the complex confirm or diverge from the BTC move? Divergence lowers conviction. There is NO

anyone testing their base?), not as a leading trigger. On weekends, books are thin and assets
decouple — down-weight weekend cross-asset reads.

Discipline — the anti-overfitting firewall

Mechanism before fit. Trial-count is the denominator. Redundant confirmations are not
confirmations (decorrelate). Forward / out-of-sample validation with a pre-committed stopping
rule. Patterns (Elliott, etc.) are admissible ONLY as falsifiable, level-based scenarios — never a
wave-count narrative.

The reversal thesis is on trial, not a belief. Grade committed calls FORWARD: is the probability
calibrated (when you say 60%, does it happen ~60%?) and does it beat a naive 50/50 baseline
on a Brier score? If it doesn't beat baseline over a meaningful sample, the thesis is dead —
retire it, don't add another conditioner to rescue it. The overfitting trap reappears at the program
level: no finite sequence of tweaks can save an idea that doesn't forward-validate.

The only thing rewarded is the objective: the 2 moves right, as precisely as possible, sharper as
the move nears. Reward outcomes, never activity. Finding more "setups" is not progress;
better-calibrated calls are.
